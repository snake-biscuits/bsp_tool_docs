So, the VIS system. What is it, and why is it called that?
`VIS` is short for `Visibility`, a core function of BSP level systems since Doom[^bisq_doom][^sage_doom]
I use the VIS abreviation because it's what the Quake compiler that handles VIS lumps is called: `vis.exe`[^idsft_vis][^ericw_vis].

VIS is in some ways, the heart of the BSP format.
It's (one of) the reason(s) we split the whole map up by planes. To pre-calculate a minimum amount of stuff to render.
Two terms that come up a lot w/ BSP are `PVS` & `PAS`. Un-abbreviated: Potentially Visible / Audible Set.
By "Set" we are referring to a collection of renderable objects, in Quake-based BSP engines these are often `Visleaves` or `Visclusters`

Visleaves are a collection of faces or mesh triangles (roughly equivalent to DOOM sectors[^bisq_doom][^sage_doom])
Visleaves are convex, so no [Renderable Primitive](https://www.khronos.org/opengl/wiki/Primitive) within the leaf overlaps another.

>This isn't entirely true, visleaves can also contain static prop geometry which needs depth sorting to render
> PVS is also used when rendering entities, which means dynamic geo (playermodels, npcs etc.)
> *Quake-based BSP Engines* do generally turn off "costly" (less so by modern standards) graphics options like depth tests & buffer clears

Visclusters are groups of visleaves, after Quake 2 **most** *Quake-based BSP Engines* have a cluster-relative PVS
That is, some lump holds a map of which clusters you can see from a given cluster[^max_pvs]
Using clusters massively reduces the number of tests needed (cluster factorial rather than leaf factorial)

Of course, the Leaves of the BSP tree alone aren't enough to map the PVS & PAS
For that you need: Portals[^jacco_prt]
