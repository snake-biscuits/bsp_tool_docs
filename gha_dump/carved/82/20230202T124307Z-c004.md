# Technical Breakdown
Within the BSP format, (most) Portals are based on `winding` objects.
`winding`s are sort of like polygons, at their most basic `winding`s are a loop of **edges**
Much like most modern hardware graphical systems, the relative orientation of a winding can be used for culling.[^bfc]

> The whole edge system is kinda convoluted & a wierd database size reduction trick we don't need to get into here
> (`.bsp` files are basically relational databases<sup>[citation needed]</sup>)

Windings can be used as stencils on the draw buffer[^sbuf], blocking future rendering.
Similar to the overdraw present in raycast (Wolf3D / DOOM) rendering algorithms.
However, portals mark a space to be drawn later.
Duke3D etc. use portals to mark spaces for sector traversal when rendering.

To aid in this process, portals often index the visleaves visible though themselves.
Using the winding of a portal, you can quickly determine which leaf in which side of the portal. (front & back)

**TL;DR: Portals are like negative polygons, they're things you don't render
And if you don't render them, you can render what you see through them**

[^bfc]: Wikipedia: [Back-face culling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back-face_culling)
[^sbuf]: Wikipedia: [Stencil buffer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stencil_buffer)
